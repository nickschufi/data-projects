# COVID-19 & World Bank Data Analysis

**Group project (team of 2)** completed during an **R-Bootcamp**.

## Objective

Build an analysis in **R Markdown** combining statistical and visual methods, with the main focus on data visualization. The goal was to find a balance between statistical analysis and suitable visuals, with the final deliverable rendered as a polished, visually appealing **HTML report**.

## Data Source

- **COVID-19** case and mortality data
- **Socio-economic indicators** from the [World Bank Open Data](https://data.worldbank.org/) portal

## Approach

1. **Data preparation & cleaning**: merged COVID-19 case/fatality data with World Bank socio-economic indicators, handled missing values and inconsistent country naming across datasets.
2. **Statistical analysis**: explored relationships between case fatality rates, unemployment, and other socio-economic indicators across countries and regions.
3. **Visualization**: built a range of static and interactive visualizations to communicate patterns across regions and indicators.
4. **Reporting**: compiled the full analysis into a single R Markdown document, rendered to HTML.

## Tools & Technologies

`R` · `R Markdown` · `tidyverse` · `dplyr` · `tidyr` · `ggplot2` · `plotly` · and additional supporting R packages

## Key Findings

- **Clear patterns** emerged between case fatality rates and unemployment, with **South America** standing out for particularly high labor market disruption.
- **Wealthier countries** generally showed stronger economic resilience during the pandemic.
- Overall, correlations between socio-economic indicators and pandemic outcomes were **weak**, showing how different the crisis was, with health, economic, and social systems interacting in ways that reject a simple one-to-one interpretation.
- The results suggest that while economic context mattered, COVID-19 health outcomes were shaped by a wide range of factors **not fully captured** in this analysis.
